Your first AI chat

You type a message. AI replies. You can ask another question, add a detail, or ask it to change something.

You do not need technical knowledge or a perfect question. Try one small thing and see what happens.

1Pick a place to try it

Choose any one of these. You do not need to compare them all or sign up for several. If you already have access to one, start there.

Start free. You do not need an upgrade for this exercise. Free access has limits; paid plans generally offer more usage or features. Consider paying later if you regularly reach a limit or want a specific feature.

Open your choice and sign in or create an account if asked. Choose the free option. If you reach a paid offer or trial screen, look for the free route. You do not need a paid trial to begin.

2Send one simple message

Find the box where you can type a message. Type or paste the example below, then click the send button, often shown as an arrow. A message you give AI is sometimes called a prompt.

Turn these notes into a friendly text message: Saturday picnic, 12:30, Oak Park, bring your own lunch, I'll bring a ball. Keep it short and don't add other details.

Wait for the answer. It might look something like this:

Hi everyone! Let's meet at Oak Park on Saturday at 12:30 for a picnic. Bring your own lunch, and I'll bring a ball!

This is an illustrative answer. Your tool may phrase it differently.

3Keep the conversation going

In the same conversation, send:

Add a question asking who can come. Otherwise keep it the same.

You can keep adjusting it in ordinary language: “Make it shorter.” “That sounds too formal.” “You missed the time.” You do not have to start over each time.

If an answer misses the point, tell it what you wanted. You have not failed at using AI.

4Read it before you use it

For this message, check the place, time, and lunch details. Does it sound like you? Copy the version you like into your own text message when you are ready.

AI can make mistakes even when it sounds certain. For important facts, check a reliable source. Asking “Are you sure?” is not a substitute for checking.

Use made-up or non-sensitive details while learning. Do not paste passwords or confidential information. For work material, use a tool your organization approves.

5Try something of your own

Choose a small task you have today. Here are three ways to begin:

  • Understand something: “Explain this in plain English and give me one example.” Then paste the words you want explained.
  • Organize your thoughts: “Turn these rough notes into a short checklist.” Then add your notes.
  • Work out where to start: “Help me think this through. Ask me one question at a time.” Then describe your situation.

Stay in the same chat for follow-ups on that task. Use “New chat,” or its equivalent, when you move to an unrelated topic. Supply the details the new conversation needs.
