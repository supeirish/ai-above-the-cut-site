# Social - Issue 013 · 2026-09-13 · "Who holds the evidence when your AI acts?"

**Live:** https://aiabovethecut.com/2026-09-13

**The strategy this week.** Everyone will post that Anthropic disclosed a fourth incident. The
number in the story is 481 million transcripts, and most posts will use it as a scale flex.
It is not one. That search did not find the fourth incident. Something else did, during a
transcript review. The 481 million was a check for others, and it came back negative.

So the post is about the gap between having records and being able to answer with them.
Anthropic had every transcript the whole time. The work was in the finding.

**The credibility post is the Corrections post, and it is the one that separates us.** We
re-opened all fifteen Margin-Proof Tracker rows at source this cycle and four of them were
wrong, including one behind a link that no longer resolves. We printed all four with the
corrected fact and a working link. Nobody else in this category does that, and this week we
did it in the same issue that argues you should ask vendors how they look for their own
mistakes. Do not bury it in a thread. Give it its own post.

Do not run the two LinkedIn posts on the same day. The primary is Sunday evening. The
Corrections post is Tuesday or Wednesday, where it can stand alone.

**Four discipline notes for every post below.**

1. **Never say the 481 million search found the fourth incident.** It did not. Anthropic's own
   words: *"After finding this incident, we broadened our search."* Getting this backwards
   inverts the whole point of the issue and is the easiest attack on us.
2. **Never imply the incidents happened in ordinary use.** All four were inside one partner's
   evaluations, where a misconfiguration connected a model to the real internet and the models
   ran without the safeguards that ship to customers. Say so in the post, not in a reply.
3. **Say what Anthropic changed.** It added a misconfigured capture-the-flag evaluation with no
   in-scope solution, hardened its training environments, and set requirements for partners. A
   post that lists the failure and skips the fix is the strawman this publication refuses.
4. **California created no audit mandate.** SB 813 and AB 1405 set up who may audit and a
   registry. Neither requires anyone to be audited to operate in the state. Do not let a
   comment thread turn this into "California now requires AI audits."

## 1 · LinkedIn - primary, post Sunday evening

Anthropic searched 481 million of its own transcripts last week.

That search did not find the incident it was looking for. Something else did, weeks earlier,
while staff were assembling transcripts to share with an outside evaluator.

The 481 million was the second pass. It was a check for anything worse. It found nothing worse.

Here is why that matters more than the number.

The records existed the whole time. Every transcript of every action. The problem was never
that the evidence was missing. The problem was turning it into an answer to a specific
question, and the first attempt, across 141,000 transcripts, missed.

Most enterprises are one incident away from learning the same thing about their own agents.

One exercise, this week. Pick one completed agent task that used an external tool. Ask its
owner to show you the original instruction, the systems it reached, what it changed, and who
approved it. Ask them to export what your team can retrieve on its own.

You will learn one of two things. Either you have a gap, or you have the capability and have
never used it. Both are worth knowing before you widen an agent's authority.
