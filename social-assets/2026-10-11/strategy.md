# Social - Issue 017 · 2026-10-11 · "The agent, not the model"

**Live:** https://aiabovethecut.com/2026-10-11

**The strategy this week.** Google introduced one AI agent for work that picks the AI model for each job, including Anthropic's Claude. Most feeds will cover it as a Gemini launch. The useful read for a leader is the shift underneath: the agent, not the model, is becoming what a company buys, and what the agent learns about your company is the part that is hard to move later.

**The second post is the money story.** OpenAI's yearly sales pace was reported at about $50 billion, not the $70 billion widely cited, and chip stocks fell. Pair it with SpaceX borrowing about $40 billion to buy Nvidia chips. Give it its own day.

Do not run the two LinkedIn posts on the same day. The primary is Monday morning. The money post is Wednesday.

**Five discipline notes for every post below.**

1. **Do not say Google's agent is generally available.** CIO Dive reports a preview for select customers. Google gave no price, and no one outside Google has tested it.
2. **Do not say OpenAI's revenue was "cut" or "restated."** The FT reported the pace near $50 billion; the higher figure came from investors comparing OpenAI with Anthropic, which counts cloud-partner sales differently. Bloomberg reported OpenAI expects $70 billion or more by year-end.
3. **Do not say the White House created a legal duty.** It said incident reporting and fixing is "not optional," and Axios said it did not make clear what enforcement or penalties would look like.
4. **Do not say AI ran the Korean bank hacks on its own.** CrowdStrike says an AI hacking tool was "likely responsible" and that such tools can let one attacker run many intrusions quickly.
5. **Do not overstate the coding study.** It is an unreviewed working paper, its data end in March 2026, and Jellyfish, which supplied the data, sells this kind of measure.

## 1 · LinkedIn - primary, post Monday morning

Google made a bet this week on how companies will use AI.

Staff stop choosing a model. They hand the work to one agent, and the agent picks the model for each job, Google's Gemini or Anthropic's Claude. Google says a task can run for days after the laptop closes, and the agent remembers the project.

If that holds, the agent, not the model, becomes what a company buys. Model makers end up competing on price inside someone else's product.

What stays put is what the agent learns about your company. That is where the switching cost sits.

So before your teams adopt a work agent, ask two things: can you set a hard cap on what it spends, and can you take what it has learned with you if you leave?

## 2 · LinkedIn - the money story, Wednesday

Two numbers from this week say a lot about the money behind AI.

$50 billion: OpenAI's yearly sales pace, about $20 billion below the figure widely cited, the FT reported. Chip stocks fell as much as 4% on the news. The labs do not count revenue the same way.

$40 billion: the debt SpaceX is seeking to buy Nvidia chips, the FT reported. That is a company borrowing to own its AI computing power, not just rent it.

Our read: much of the AI buildout is financed against a few labs' revenue claims. If you rely on any of those figures, ask how they were counted.
