## 1 · LinkedIn newsletter companion (Sunday night)

**Headline:** Anthropic opened its books. The meter is how the bill reaches you.

**Subtitle:** What jumped out at me from this week's AI news, and the full list of headlines.

*AI Above the Cut is my free weekly newsletter on the AI news that matters for business leaders. Subscribe at aiabovethecut.com/subscribe.*

This is my quick editorial on what rose above the cut this week. One story stood out. Anthropic, the maker of Claude, filed to go public on Monday, October 26. It is the first full look at the money inside a top AI lab.

My take in one line: **Your AI vendor has bills fixed for years and revenue that moves with use. That makes your usage, and who watches it, the thing to manage.**

Three parts:

1. **What the filing shows.** Fast growth and one profitable quarter.
2. **Fixed bills, flexible revenue.** The compute deals run for years. Most revenue moves with use.
3. **Where the bill lands.** On the meter, and on whoever watches it.

At the end, a quick inventory of everything in this week's issue.

[Heading] What the filing shows

Anthropic reported $16.3 billion in revenue for the first half of 2026. A year earlier, it was $1.2 billion.

Its operating loss was $693 million for the half. But the second quarter alone made a $974 million operating profit. That came after five straight quarterly losses.

Its net loss was $80.7 billion. Most of that was an accounting charge, not cash.

Customers who paid Anthropic a year ago now spend about nine times as much as a group, it says. That figure leaves out its fixed-price Pro and Max plans.

Its seven co-founders will keep 50.1% of the vote. ([Anthropic S-1](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm))

[Heading] Fixed bills, flexible revenue

The filing says Anthropic's revenue is "primarily consumption-based," and much of it is not under long-term contract. Its compute deals "generally extend over multiple years," on terms that "obligate us to pay for resources regardless of actual utilization." The Register puts those obligations at more than half a trillion dollars.

Meeting those bills "depends on our ability to generate significant revenue growth," the filing warns.

Some of that risk is passing to buyers. "A growing number of enterprise customers" now sign non-cancelable contracts with minimum spend, the filing says.

Lenders are paying attention too. Blackstone, the anchor investor in a $63 billion deal to finance Anthropic's chips, expects Anthropic to get a "junk" credit rating after the listing, the FT reported. ([Anthropic S-1](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/26/humanity-cant-afford-not-to-spend-800b-on-ai-anthropic-insists/5305580) · [TechCrunch on the FT](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/anthropic-expected-to-receive-high-junk-rating-from-credit-agencies-following-ipo-report-says/))

[Heading] Where the bill lands

Here is my read. **A vendor with fixed costs needs your usage to grow. The meter is how the bill reaches you.**

The rest of the week pointed the same way:

• **Investors want proof.** Alphabet lost roughly $500 billion in stock market value on Thursday, October 29. Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft plan to spend more than $740 billion on capital this year, TechCrunch reported. Investors sold. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/cracks-are-emerging-in-the-ai-spending-boom/))
• **Few buyers are beating their targets yet.** Just 3% of organizations exceed their cost-savings goals through productivity, Gartner says. Yet 85% plan to spend more next year. ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-26-gartner-hr-research-finds-77-percent-of-board-members-expect-ai-to-deliver-productivity-and-cost-savings-yet-just-3-percent-of-organizations-exceed-cost-savings-goals-via-productivity))
• **More AI is moving to a meter.** GitHub now includes its Copilot coding assistant with GitHub Enterprise seats for customers who work with an account team. Use is "metered from the first request against your enterprise budget." When the budget runs out, chat and code review stop. Oracle will charge for AI work on top of its subscriptions. ([GitHub](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-10-28-github-enterprise-now-includes-access-to-github-copilot) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/applications/2026/10/29/oracle-wants-to-bill-you-for-every-ai-task-not-just-every-seat/5306403))

The number I keep coming back to is **nine times**. That is great for the seller. For a buyer, it is good only if the work is worth more than the bill. Many companies cannot yet tell.

I think this is where the **Citizen CEO** idea meets the budget. As AI comes built into the tools people already use, more people can direct it, and usage grows from the ground up. Someone still has to own the meter.

So, in my view, three questions now belong to leadership, not only to procurement:

• What are we billed on: seats, usage or results, and what minimum have we promised?
• Who on our side watches the meter, and what caps it?
• How fast could we switch, and what would it cost?

[Heading] The fair counter-case

Buyers may have more power than this suggests. OpenAI says its new mid-priced model, GPT-6.1 Terra, costs half as much as the one before it. OpenAI is reportedly offering discounts of more than 30% to win Anthropic's customers, The Information reported, though TNW said it had not verified the figures. And Anthropic's revenue ran nearly double its compute costs in the first half, its filing shows. So there may be room to bargain. ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-discounts-anthropic-customers-replit-rippling) · [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/26/openai-rolls-out-budget-priced-mid-tier-gpt-61-terra/5305574))

A meter can also protect you. You pay for work done, not for seats no one uses. The danger is not the meter itself. It is a meter no one on your side is watching.

The question I'm left with: **if your AI vendor needs your usage to grow, who on your side decides how much it grows?**

## Reply preparation (LinkedIn comments)

**"So Anthropic is profitable now?"** One quarter was. The second quarter alone made a $974 million operating profit, after five straight quarterly losses. For the half, the operating loss was $693 million. The $80.7 billion net loss was mostly an accounting charge, not cash.

**"Isn't this just an Anthropic ad?"** Anthropic is the lead because it filed to go public, and the issue says the newsletter uses Anthropic and OpenAI models. The figures are from its own filing. The same filing warns that meeting its compute bills depends on significant revenue growth, and Blackstone expects a "junk" credit rating after the listing, the FT reported.

**"Does nine times mean my bill will go up ninefold?"** No. It is a group figure for customers who paid Anthropic a year ago, and it leaves out fixed-price Pro and Max plans. The point is that a seller with fixed costs needs usage to grow, so someone on the buyer's side should watch it.

**"Are usage-based contracts a bad deal?"** Not on their own. A meter can protect you: you pay for work done, not for seats no one uses. The danger is a meter no one on your side is watching.

**"Is Microsoft cutting jobs because of AI?"** It offered buyouts to about 60,000 employees, about 27% of its staff, AP reported. It is an offer, not a layoff, and Microsoft's own post does not mention AI. The issue's read: redesign the work first, then decide the headcount.

**"What do you mean by Citizen CEO?"** An ordinary person able to direct a team of AI agents, each with its own role. This week, that showed up as tools staff can switch on themselves, and the question of who owns what those tools reach and what they cost.
