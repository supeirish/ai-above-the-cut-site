## 1 · LinkedIn - primary, post Monday morning

This week I wrote our newsletter's first editorial. Here is the short version.

For two years the story was what AI could do. The models are now good enough for a great deal of everyday work. The hard part is packaging: making AI simple enough that people actually hand it work.

Look at what shipped. Anthropic folded Cowork and Claude Design into one Claude chat. Meta's Muse remembers what matters to you, shops and books for you, and now helps small businesses run their ads and books. OpenAI's dots are always-on agents you name and give your goals.

I use AI every day, and it is still hard to use well. It produces more than I can take in. So the packaging matters.

But I would argue the shift comes from something bigger: what I call the Synthetic Space. It is a working room where a business sets up synthetic roles, AI agents each with a defined job, and where artificial and organic (i.e., human) workers share context and create together.

Pieces are arriving. Whoever unlocks it first, in a meaningful way, will set the direction of the market.

## 2 · LinkedIn - the money story, Wednesday

A large call-center company says the AI it installs for clients is shrinking its own revenue.

Concentrix runs customer service for other companies. It expects fourth-quarter revenue to fall 3% to 5%, before currency moves. On its earnings call, its chief executive said the automations went in "much faster than what we originally expected." Its finance chief also named clients cutting support, and neither split the causes.

Our read: when a vendor automates work you pay for by the hour, the saving can come back to you as a smaller bill.

If you buy outsourced service work, ask this quarter: as your vendor automates, does your price fall with it?

## 5 · What not to post this week

- **"OpenAI shelved its best model."** OpenAI told The Register it held back GPT-6.1 Astra, the model it had planned for October. It did not call it its best.
- **"California says AI companies are liable for everything."** The law removes one defense, "the AI did it on its own." Other defenses remain.
- **The Gartner 70% as fact.** It is a forecast with no published method, from a firm that sells advice on these deals.
