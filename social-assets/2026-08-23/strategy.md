# Social — Issue 010 · 2026-08-23 · "Google bid $10 million for a failed airline's internal memory."

**Live:** https://aiabovethecut.com/2026-08-23

**The strategy this week.** The hook is a single reversal: everyone assumes the valuable data in a
bankruptcy is the customer list. In this sale the customer list was excluded, and the buyers wanted
the staff email. That inverts the reader's instinct, and an inverted instinct is the most shareable
thing a brief can offer.

Lead with what was in the schedule. Resolve into the executive question: **if this company were sold
tomorrow, which line would a buyer actually bid for?**

The credibility post is separate and stronger than usual: we were one step from adding a company to
our own Tracker on the strength of a headline, opened the filing instead, and found the AI
attribution had been written by an AI. That post says the scorecard is real.

Do not run both on the same day. Spirit is Sunday evening or Monday morning. The KE Holdings post is
Tuesday or Wednesday, where it can stand alone.

**One discipline note for every post below:** the sale is **not approved**. Never write "sold" or

## 1 · LinkedIn — primary, post Sunday evening

A bankrupt airline put its data up for auction this month.

Everyone assumes the prize in a sale like that is the customer list.

It was excluded. All of it. Ninety-seven million customer profiles, fifty million loyalty members, thirty million recorded service calls, every one marked "Not Included" on the schedule.

Here is what the buyers actually wanted.

100 million emails across 80,000 staff mailboxes. 500 million Teams messages. SharePoint. 516 code repositories. Crew rosters. Fuel slips.

The exhaust. The record of how the company actually ran.

Google's winning bid was $10 million. A second bidder was at $7.5 million. A third company came in afterwards at $12.5 million, too late for the auction.

Three buyers, one asset class, and none of them wanted the customer database.

**You already know your customer data has a market value. This says your internal operating record has one too.**

Most companies govern the first and have never once looked at the second.

So here is the question worth putting to your general counsel this week: if we were sold or wound up tomorrow, what would be on our data asset schedule?

Not the CRM. The mailboxes, the chat archive, the ticket system, the repositories, the training records.

Most people have never seen that list for their own company.

Nothing has been sold yet, by the way. A judge adjourned the approval hearing to September 9 after the flight attendants' union objected that you cannot strip the names out and keep the links between records and still call it anonymous.

That question is now in front of a court. The answer will travel well beyond one airline.

## 2 · LinkedIn — credibility post, Tuesday or Wednesday

We nearly put a company on our scorecard this week on the strength of a headline.

The headline said a company had credited its best margins in three years to AI. Profit up 74.9%, margin at a three-year high. Real numbers, real company, real quarter.

It would have been our thirteenth row.

We opened the earnings release first, because that is the rule.

Asked directly what drove profit past revenue, the CFO named three things: a lower cost baseline, operating efficiency, and business mix.

AI was not among them. Not in the call. Not in the written release.

So where did the AI attribution come from?

The standfirst of a third-party transcript page. And that page's own footer says parts of the article "were created using Large Language Models."

**An AI-written line invented an AI success story the company never told.**

We did not add the row.

This is a small thing that is about to stop being small. AI-generated summaries are entering the record as claims, and the next person to repeat that claim will cite the summary, not the filing.

The only defence is boring and it does not scale: open the document.

## 6 · Friday split — the narrow disagreement

If you can fund only one this year, do you build the system or preserve the corpus?

A system is what makes the material usable, and Deloitte's survey suggests most leaders do not yet
feel prepared to redesign around agents. But a corpus accumulates in calendar time and only to
whoever was doing the work.

Our read: fund the system, and start preserving now. And the honest limit, from our own lead item:
Spirit shows a rival can sometimes just buy a corpus.
