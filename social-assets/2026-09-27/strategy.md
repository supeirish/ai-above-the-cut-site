## 1 · LinkedIn - primary, post Monday morning

A federal appeals court just let the Pentagon drop an AI supplier over what its model
refuses to do.

Anthropic would not relax contract bans on using Claude for lethal autonomous warfare or
mass domestic surveillance. The department said Anthropic's control of Claude's safeguards
could let it limit military use. Under a 2018 purchasing law, it ordered Claude out within
180 days.

Two of three judges agreed, holding that building refusals into a model can count as
"manipulating" it, the law's own word. One judge dissented.

The ruling covers one federal purchasing law, not commercial contracts. But the point
travels.

What a model refuses is part of what you buy. So is who can change it. The court notes that
Anthropic can change how Claude behaves with each new model.

If your business depends on an AI tool, the terms worth having in writing are notice before
its behavior or usage policy changes, the right to test each new version on your own work,
time on the old version, and a way out.

## 2 · LinkedIn - the platform shift, Wednesday

The AI assistant your staff open is turning into a helper that keeps working while they are
away.

On September 16, Anthropic began merging Claude's chat with Cowork, its mode for handing
over whole tasks. By default it asks before it acts.

On September 25, Microsoft announced a Home view in Copilot that brings Chat together with
its own Cowork mode. Its Autopilot agent, which "keeps working even when you're not," goes
to a private preview at the end of the month.

Meta's Muse agent, launched September 8, also keeps working after the app is closed. The
app-data firm Apptopia estimates 2.8 million installs in its first 12 days.

One detail matters for budgets. Microsoft bills Cowork, Autopilot and its new Code feature
by usage, not through the per-user license that covers everyday chat.

So the terms that matter now include which accounts the assistant may act in, whether it
asks first, and what the handed-over work costs.

## 5 · What not to post this week

- **"OpenAI hacked Medicare."** The issue says unauthorized access to a Services Australia
  statistics portal, with no personal information believed accessed so far.
- **Any claim that the ruling applies to your vendor contracts.** It covers one federal
  purchasing law. The contract advice stands on its own; do not borrow the court's authority
  for it.
- **The Muse install number as a verdict on Meta.** It is a third-party estimate after 12
  days. It says the app drew downloads, not that it will hold users.
- **The VA slowdown as fact.** The five-day figure is the fellow's allegation. The court
  ruled on jurisdiction only.
- **The price cuts as a price war winner.** Anthropic and OpenAI priced new models below the
  ones they replace, each against a different basis. Cheaper per token is not necessarily
  cheaper per task.

## 6 · Reply preparation

**"You went easy on Anthropic."** The lead reports the loss in its first sentence, gives the
department's stated reason, and gives the dissent one line. The ruling is narrow, and the
issue says so because the opinion says so, not to soften it.

**"This is just the Pentagon punishing a company for having values."** The issue does not
take that side or the other. The department's reason and Anthropic's position are both in
the piece. The buyer's point holds either way: the limits you depend on can change, so get
the terms in writing.

**"Agents that keep working when I'm away are a security risk."** That is the point of the
post. Which accounts it may act in and whether it asks first are the terms to set before
rollout, not after.

**"Usage billing is how vendors make more money."** Microsoft's own post splits it: a fixed
per-user license for everyday chat, usage billing for delegated work. That is a budgeting
fact to plan for, not a verdict on the pricing.
