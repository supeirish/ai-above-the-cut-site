**Discipline notes for each piece below.**

1. **Not attacks.** Officials told Congress on November 18 that the outages were not attacks. The FBI saw "no indication whatsoever" of malicious activity. Credit the officials, not us.
2. **"At least some."** At least some outages came from rushing to patch AI-found flaws, CIO Dive reported. Never say all of them. Microsoft blames its own November 12 to 13 outage on a network upgrade, not AI. Say so whenever Microsoft's outage comes up.
3. **5x to 10x is an estimate.** It is Citi chief executive Jane Fraser's estimate ("five-, tenfold"), from a Bloomberg interview reported by Banking Dive. Not a count.
4. **Anthropic's IPO:** priced at $83 on November 18, above the $70 to $78 range. $119.5 billion counts the IPO, a private sale of shares and shares sold by existing holders, before fees. Don't call it "what Anthropic raised." No share count.
5. **$26.0 billion** came from seven tech companies in the private sale. Anthropic has deals to buy from all of them except TSMC (TechCrunch).
6. **$900 billion** is spending commitments, mostly for computing (TechCrunch, citing a filing). Some agreements can be ended early. Not "promised to spend."
7. **Insurance:** 1% of 100 insurance CEOs report a measurable return on AI. 64% plan to put 11% to 20% of their budget into AI. Credit KPMG's survey.
8. **Disclosure:** Anthropic and OpenAI models help make the newsletter, and this issue covers Anthropic's IPO and OpenAI's models. Piece 1 says so once.

## 1 · LinkedIn newsletter companion (Sunday night)

**Headline:** AI found the holes. Fixing them took systems down.

**Subtitle:** What stood out to me this week, and the full list of headlines.

*AI Above the Cut is my free weekly newsletter on the AI news that matters for business leaders. Subscribe at aiabovethecut.com/subscribe.*

[Heading] What happened

Over eight days, outages hit Microsoft, the FAA, JPMorgan, United Airlines, Apple and the National Security Agency. On November 18, officials told Congress these were not attacks. The FBI saw "no indication whatsoever" of malicious activity.

At least some of the outages came from rushing to patch flaws that AI found, CIO Dive reported. Microsoft says its own outage came from a network upgrade, not AI.

[Heading] Why it caught my eye

Citi's chief executive, Jane Fraser, estimates the patching now going through the world's tech systems is "five-, tenfold" anything seen before. A lawyer at Mayer Brown put the gap plainly: "The capacity to find vulnerabilities has grown much faster than the capacity to fix them."

Fraser expects more outages for a while. She also says the patching "will make us all a lot more resilient."

My read: the cost of AI-found flaws now includes downtime, not only break-ins. Ask your critical vendors how they test urgent patches.

[Heading] This week's headlines

- AI found the holes. The rush to fix them took systems down.
- Anthropic priced the largest IPO on record, above its range.
- Nvidia's sales doubled. It says it will be choosier about funding its customers.
- Microsoft put Word, Excel and PowerPoint inside Copilot.
- KPMG ran an audit with more than 100 AI agents.
- Citi says customers carry the loss when a third-party AI agent goes wrong.
- Attackers are using AI on the same patches.
- The newest jobs data on young workers is less tidy than the story.
- Is the AI money real, or circular?
