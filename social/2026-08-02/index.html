The 15 minutes before you post

The least glamorous task on the list is often the highest-leverage. Be useful in the conversation before asking the conversation to notice you.

Spend 15 minutes before and 20 minutes after each main post.
Leave five real comments: two interpreters, two operators, and one source cited this week.
Choose posts from the last 2–4 hours with a real argument and room for a useful response.
Write 40–80 words: name their point, add the fact only you have, and leave a narrow opening.
Never lead with your newsletter link. The comment earns the profile click; the profile does the selling.

TUESDAY — LinkedIn main post

No imageNo follow-up comment
Post copy

Press put Visa's cut last week at about 2,600 roles. Visa's own numbers for the same quarter: $563M of severance, revenue up 14%, and raised guidance.

That combination is the story, not the number.

Visa said the majority of the eliminated roles were in technology and product, the function that builds what it sells. The CEO described teams of ten or more becoming "agentic squads of two to four." For teams using the agentic toolchain he cited 80% more code commits.

Asked where the savings go, he named a long list. Stablecoins and agentic commerce were on it: mechanisms by which a payment could one day settle without touching a card network at all, or become layers Visa orchestrates.

Visa did not put a number on AI's share of the reduction. We searched EDGAR full text for the exact phrase "AI capital expenditures," all form types, 26 July to 2 August: zero results, second week running.

Our read: a company beating and raising shrank the function that builds its product and aimed part of the proceeds at its own disintermediation risk. The CFO said margins should hold.

For any AI-enabled cut on your plan: what is the freed budget pointed at, who owns it, by when? Funding only margin is an efficiency case. Funding a named bet is an option.

THURSDAY — the second cut (LinkedIn)

No imageNo follow-up comment
Post copy

Anthropic reviewed 141,006 evaluation runs and found three cases where third-party evaluation containment failed and model activity reached real production systems.

In one, a model published a booby-trapped package to the live PyPI registry. It ran on 15 real systems.

The detail that should matter to you: the two organisations Anthropic reached had not detected the activity themselves.

Anthropic is explicit that the model did not deliberately escape. It calls this "closer to a harness and operational failure than a model alignment failure," which is a meaningfully different thing from OpenAI's separately disclosed zero-day escape.

Both labs published first-party incident reports. This is an event on the record, not a read of ours. Anthropic's write-up is also candid about what it could not determine, which is rarer than it should be.

Your third-party AI risk register probably lists your model vendors. It almost certainly does not list your model vendor's evaluation vendor.

That is the gap this week exposed.

If you own vendor risk: does your assessment reach past the vendor to the parties they test with?

#AI #CISO #VendorRisk

FRIDAY — Converse (LinkedIn)

No imageNo follow-up comment
Post copy

1,289 characters. The real split from Where the Minds Disagree, ending in a narrow question to a named function.

Visa named stablecoins and agentic commerce on its reinvestment list last week, in the same quarter it booked $563M of severance and raised guidance.

There are two readings of that, and they point opposite ways.

Read one is disintermediation. Stablecoin rails can settle value without a four-party card network in the path. On that view Visa is buying position against its own obsolescence, and defensive spending rarely earns a premium.

Read two is absorption. Visa's announced work lets partners settle with Visa in stablecoins, folding the new infrastructure into things Visa already owns: acceptance, brand, compliance, dispute handling. The new rails become another layer Visa orchestrates rather than a bypass.

Our read is absorption. The scarce assets in payments look like acceptance and trust rather than settlement mechanics.

Here is where we could be wrong, stated in advance so you can hold us to it. The ambition is visible in the spend, not in the results. What would move us off absorption is evidence of stablecoin volume settling outside the network at scale.

For anyone in payments or treasury: when you model settlement moving off-card, what share do you assume actually leaves the network, versus getting re-intermediated one layer up?

#AI #Payments #Stablecoins

DAILY — X replies (~20 min)

No imageNo follow-up comment
Post copy

X is a replying platform here, not a posting platform. Conversation depth outranks passive engagement, and it sidesteps the link penalty.

This week's reply topic: what the freed budget gets pointed at. Where to be useful rather than promotional:

- Payments and fintech threads on the Visa cut. Most will quote the ~2,600 figure as fact. The useful reply is the scope: that number appears in no Visa document, while the $563M severance and the "majority in technology and product" scope do. Correct the provenance, do not pitch.
- AI-productivity threads. The St. Louis Fed corpus is the highest-value thing to hand someone: ~490,000 transcripts, 5,198 firms, and roughly 95% of AI-related productivity sentences point at future gains versus about three-fourths of non-AI ones.
- AI security and vendor-risk threads. The Anthropic and OpenAI incident reports are first-party and unusually specific. Link the primary, not our coverage.
- EU AI Act threads. The Article 50 grace period to 2 December covers only the 50(2) content-marking duty. That distinction is being flattened almost everywhere and correcting it is genuinely useful.
