Standing strategy

Turn one proprietary weekly finding into executive attention, useful conversation, and qualified newsletter readers.

Operating rule

Useful before promotional

Each post should deliver the core value in the feed. The newsletter link is the next step, not the price of admission.

Weekly rule

One issue, several distinct objects

Do not compress the same caption across channels. Give each surface the form its audience can save, share, discuss, or use.

Post shapes

Reusable forms that keep the week coherent without making every post identical.

The proprietary finding

A precise claim, the number or filing behind it, and what an operator should do differently.

The evidence object

A chart, tracker, scorecard, or annotated excerpt that earns a save without needing the newsletter.

The split

Two credible positions, the hidden assumption separating them, and one narrow question.

The decision test

A reusable question or checklist readers can take into a meeting this week.

The 15 minutes before you post

The least glamorous task on the list is often the highest-leverage. Be useful in the conversation before asking the conversation to notice you.

Spend 15 minutes before and 20 minutes after each main post.
Leave five real comments: two interpreters, two operators, and one source cited this week.
Choose posts from the last 2–4 hours with a real argument and room for a useful response.
Write 40–80 words: name their point, add the fact only you have, and leave a narrow opening.
Never lead with your newsletter link. The comment earns the profile click; the profile does the selling.

LinkedIn — 1,169 characters

No imageNo follow-up comment
Post copy

ServiceNow's gross margin fell from 80% to 73.5%. Customer AI adoption is one of the two reasons it named.

That is the precise part.

Four large companies reported inside eight days. Every one put an audited number on what AI cost. Not one put an audited number on what AI earned.

Alphabet: $44.9B on facilities and equipment, free cash flow negative $5.9B.
IBM: gen-AI is about half of Consulting signings, and it cut its growth forecast anyway.
Infosys: 8.2% of revenue now labelled "AI," full-year guidance cut to 1.5–3%.

Signings, backlog, contract value. None of those touch an audited statement.

Then we ran the filings. Every 8-K filed July 19–26: exactly one pairs "artificial intelligence" with "restructuring plan." Across all forms, zero say "AI capital expenditures."

Companies tell reporters AI is reshaping their workforce. They tell their regulator something vaguer. Only one of those carries liability.

Which side of your P&L does AI show up on with a number attached?

If you sit in FP&A or IR: what would it take to isolate an AI revenue number your auditor would sign?

Eleven companies have told the market AI is paying off. We scored every one of them against the same evidence ladder.

Nobody is above rung 2.

Rung 3 means AI isolated in reported results. Rung 4 means it held for four straight quarters. Ten of the eleven sit at rung 2 — a dollar figure asserted, never reconciled — and one sits lower.

Two rows moved down this week. Issue 005 graded S&P Global and Ecolab at Stage 3; both are Stage 2. Their claim traced to a bank's review of earnings calls, not to their own filings. We marked them down. They report Tuesday and can earn it back.

The empty column is the finding.

Slide 8 is the version you can run on your own AI spend: four questions, and what the answers mean for whether you are funding a cost line or building an advantage.

Wednesday second cut — the insurance line (LinkedIn)

No imageNo follow-up comment
Post copy

CG 40 47. CG 40 48. CG 35 08.

Three endorsement forms your broker has not mentioned yet.

They carve generative-AI injury out of standard general liability cover. One carrier now runs an absolute AI exclusion across D&O, professional and fiduciary lines.

Refusing to cover a risk does not remove it. It moves it onto the balance sheet of whoever deployed the tool. The vendor keeps the margin. The customer keeps the liability.

Ask your broker before renewal, while you still have leverage.

If you own risk or GC: has your carrier raised an AI exclusion at renewal yet, and did they price it or just add it?
