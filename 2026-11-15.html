01 · The One Thing
AI agents are knocking. Companies are deciding whether to open the door.

On November 13, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet with American Express, Mastercard and Visa, first through dots for US Pro subscribers. An agent pays with a stand-in card number, and the user approves each purchase with a check such as Face ID. OpenAI says websites need to make sure agents "are not inadvertently blocked." The same week, United said it will restrict outside AI agents, citing risks like unintended purchases. JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America and Citi have blocked agents from customer accounts, Banking Dive reported. The traffic is still tiny. Airbnb's chief executive called agent bookings "a very non-material number." The fight now is over the rules, not the volume. (OpenAI)

  The executive shift: Companies now have to decide whether AI agents get in. United said no this week, and the biggest US banks already block them (TechCrunch · Banking Dive). OpenAI and the card networks are building a way to say yes, with the customer approving each purchase.
  The sticking point is proof. Knowing an agent works for a customer doesn't show that a given payment was approved, lawyers at WilmerHale note (PYMNTS). Insurers are pulling back too: state filings show 2,369 generative AI exclusion records across 49 states, mostly in general liability policies (Insurance Business).

Why it matters: those who overspent averaged 24% over, CFO Dive reported. AI's bill is now a finance problem. (CFO Dive)
03Product
Anthropic shipped a set of office tools in one week (Nov 10 to 12). Claude Sheets and Whiteboards are in beta, and Claude Data is out for paid plans. Claude Meetings, a beta for Team and Enterprise, joins Google Meet calls as a visible participant and writes notes. It is not yet offered for HIPAA-ready or zero-retention setups. Apps such as Adobe, Atlassian and Box now open inside Claude, where you or Claude can edit the work. Claude Opus 5.5 is now the default model on Pro, Max, Ultra and Team plans. For leaders: the AI assistant is turning into an office suite. Claude wants to be where employees do their work. ChatGPT wants to be where customers decide what to buy. (Anthropic · Anthropic on Meetings · Anthropic on apps · Anthropic on Opus 5.5)
Microsoft will turn on Claude by default for UK Copilot customers on December 9, The Register reported. Anthropic processes that data in the US. For leaders: if you run Copilot in the UK, check your settings before that date. (The Register)
Google put its newest model, Gemini 4 Hydrogen, into Search worldwide (Nov 12), and let US Gemini app users connect Google Wallet (Nov 13). For leaders: the assistants want to see what people spend, which is the step before buying for them. (Google · Google on Wallet)
OpenAI opened Businesses in ChatGPT in beta (Nov 10). Companies can claim a profile, see engagement and offer an agent that customers can talk to, starting with some US online businesses. For leaders: this is the other half of the wallet. OpenAI wants businesses to sell inside ChatGPT, not only on their own sites. (OpenAI)
04Do This Week
1 · United and the big banks shut AI agents out, while OpenAI is asking sites to let them in. Decide what agents may do on your website, app and phone lines, and who signs off. Owner: your chief digital officer, with legal and fraud. Our read: one simple split is to let agents look things up, while payments, bookings and account changes wait for the customer's approval (see 01 · The One Thing).
2 · State filings show 2,369 generative AI exclusions in insurance policies across 49 states. Ask your broker whether your policies now leave out AI, and what happens if an agent acting for you makes a mistake. Owner: your chief risk officer, with the CFO. Our read: exclusions can shrink coverage at renewal without anyone noticing.
3 · Stolen AI keys ran up about $740,000 in charges at one cloud platform. Take stock of the AI service keys your teams hold, and put a spending cap on each. Owner: your chief information security officer. Our read: a stolen key is a bill, not only a breach (see 05 · The Signal).
05The Signal
Horizon  Nvidia's founder is out, and AI's build-out may lose its banker. Primary: Nvidia. Reported: TechCrunch, Axios, CIO Dive. Nvidia announced Huang's resignation on November 12, and finance chief Colette Kress is interim CEO. It followed reports of an alleged affair with a woman suspected of spying for China, TechCrunch reported. Nvidia shares fell 4.3% that day, Axios reported. Companies it backs fell more: CoreWeave 6.9% and Nebius 16.8%. For the money Nvidia puts into AI, see 02 · Three Numbers. Forrester's advice, via CIO Dive: don't overreact, but check how much of your AI plan rests on one supplier. Our read: Nvidia's role as a financier is the new uncertainty to watch. Investors already treated the companies it backs as more exposed than Nvidia itself. (Nvidia · TechCrunch · Axios · CIO Dive)
Proof  Anthropic set its IPO terms. Investors want in. Primary: Anthropic. Reported: CNBC, The Verge; Bloomberg via summary. Anthropic is offering 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, to list on Nasdaq as ANT. At the top of the range, it would be valued at as much as $1.4 trillion, The Verge reported, up from $965 billion in its May funding round. The offering is about six times oversubscribed, Bloomberg reported. Anthropic confirmed trading should start November 19, The Verge reported. Its chief executive called finding computing power the company's "deepest business dilemma," Bloomberg reported. Our read: demand for the shares looks strong. The question buyers face after the listing is the one Amodei named: the cost of compute. (Anthropic · CNBC · The Verge · Bloomberg)
Deploy  AI bills are running ahead of AI results. Reported: CFO Dive, Axios, American Banker, CIO Dive. In Capgemini's survey, about 60% of executives said AI spending ran well above plan, and 21% went more than 30% over, CFO Dive reported. Executives told Axios they have not worked out how to turn AI tools into company-wide gains. Citi is redesigning about 100 processes around AI, American Banker reported, while most banks in its research are adding AI to old workflows. Northern Trust says an onboarding agent built with Microsoft engineers cut analysts' data preparation time 83%, from two and a half weeks to three days. That is the bank's own figure. Our read: the one clear gain reported this week, Northern Trust's time savings, came from rebuilding a workflow, not buying more seats. (CFO Dive · Axios · American Banker · CIO Dive)
Trust  Your AI risk now includes your vendors' vendors. Reported: Forbes, TechCrunch, The Hacker News, Ars Technica. Microsoft's email and Copilot went down worldwide for more than a day starting November 12. The outage also hit companies that rely on Microsoft, including Anthropic and OpenAI, Forbes reported. Microsoft blamed "a technical issue not related to cyber or AI." Hackers got into Fireworks AI, a service developers use to run open models, through its cloud provider. Notion said about 1% of its customers lost data, including some Notion AI chat transcripts. At Railway, a hosting platform, AI keys stolen in an October breach ran up an estimated $740,000 in unauthorized charges, the company said. It now says it will pay charges AI providers won't refund. And researchers at Apiiro found thousands of passwords and tokens in Claude Code settings files posted to public GitHub projects. Our read: your AI tools depend on systems, keys and data your security team may not track. (Forbes · TechCrunch · The Hacker News · Ars Technica)
▼ Below the Cut
AI is wiping out jobs, the story goes. Gartner's numbers are messier. Primary: Gartner. For 2026, Gartner's AI job estimates show 2.2 million jobs lost and 1.6 million gained, plus 1.3 million positions that executives put off creating. By 2030 it forecasts 10.6 million jobs created and 8.9 million removed, a net gain of 1.7 million. Those are counts of jobs. Pope Leo XIV raised a different question the same week, about what AI means for the value of work: "an algorithm cannot replace human discernment or a worker's value." Our read: this year, AI is cutting jobs, delaying hires and creating new ones all at once. The 2030 gain is a forecast, not a count. (Gartner · Pope Leo XIV on X · his Buenos Aires address)

07The Synthesis
Who controls the permission

The next challenge for AI agents is not whether they can act. It's setting what they may do, enforcing that limit, and proving what happened when something goes wrong.

Authority. OpenAI's wallet has the customer approve each purchase and keeps a record. United cites unintended purchases and account changes, and banks cite liability and identity checks (see 01 · The One Thing).
Enforcement. A stolen AI key works for whoever holds it. Railway and Notion show how far keys and transcripts travel once they leave your hands (see 05 · The Signal).
Accountability. Insurers are adding generative AI exclusions to general liability policies, so someone else has to carry that risk (see 04 · Do This Week).

Analysis: the contest may be over who controls discovery and starts the purchase, not only who builds the smartest agent. Under OpenAI's wallet, businesses keep their customer relationships, prices, delivery and service, OpenAI says. But if shoppers start in an assistant, the assistant shapes what they see and what they buy. Card networks and banks keep a central role, because they already approve payments and settle disputes. Merchants gain a new channel but depend more on the platforms that send them customers.

The counter-case. Agents may not matter much for a while. Uber's chief executive said ordering a ride takes a median of 50 seconds, so few people need an agent for it, Fortune reported. If customers don't come, the new rules will sit unused. (Fortune)

08Where the Minds Disagree
 
Will AI agents become a real way to buy?

The case that they will. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week, and many use it to research what to buy. Its wallet and business tools are built to close that loop. Salesforce projects AI will help drive $75.8 billion of Cyber Week spending, up 13%. That counts spending AI influences, such as recommendations, not only purchases agents make. (OpenAI · Salesforce)

The case that they won't, soon. Salesforce's own survey found 49% of shoppers don't want agents browsing or buying for them at all. Uber, DoorDash and Airbnb all told investors they see little agent traffic, Fortune reported. PNC's chief executive says the industry is "years away" because no one has settled who is liable, Banking Dive reported. (Fortune · Banking Dive)

Our read: both sides agree a person should approve the purchase for now. They disagree on how fast that limit relaxes. OpenAI expects pre-approved spending limits over time. The banks want liability rules first.
